Understanding AI and Large Language Models

Published on February 20, 2026 by Alex Chen

What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence refers to systems designed to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence. These tasks include understanding natural language, recognizing patterns, making decisions, and generating creative content. While AI research dates back to the 1950s, recent breakthroughs in deep learning have accelerated progress at an unprecedented pace.

The Rise of Large Language Models

Large Language Models (LLMs) are a class of AI systems trained on vast amounts of text data. By learning statistical patterns across billions of words, these models develop a remarkable ability to generate coherent text, answer questions, translate languages, and even write code. Models like GPT, Claude, Gemini, and LLaMA have demonstrated capabilities that were considered science fiction just a decade ago.

The "large" in LLM refers to both the size of the training data and the number of parameters in the model. Modern LLMs can have hundreds of billions of parameters, each one a tiny numerical weight that the model adjusts during training to better predict the next word in a sequence.

How LLMs Work

At their core, LLMs use a neural network architecture called the Transformer, introduced in 2017. Transformers use a mechanism called "attention" that allows the model to weigh the importance of different words in a sentence relative to each other. This lets the model understand context over long passages of text.

Training an LLM involves two main phases. First, pre-training exposes the model to enormous text datasets, teaching it grammar, facts, and reasoning patterns. Then, fine-tuning aligns the model with human preferences through techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), making it more helpful and safer to use.

On-Device AI

A growing trend in AI is running models directly on user devices rather than in the cloud. Smaller, optimized models like Gemini Nano can run entirely in the browser or on a smartphone, enabling AI features without sending data to external servers. This approach offers significant advantages for privacy, latency, and offline availability.

Browser APIs like the Prompt API bring on-device AI to the web platform, allowing developers to build intelligent features that work without API keys or backend infrastructure. This is the approach that ArionTalk uses to power its voice conversations entirely on-device.

Challenges and Limitations

Despite their impressive capabilities, LLMs face several challenges. They can generate plausible-sounding but incorrect information, a phenomenon known as "hallucination." They also have knowledge cutoff dates, meaning they lack information about recent events. Bias in training data can lead to biased outputs, and the computational cost of training large models raises environmental concerns.

Researchers are actively working on solutions: retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) helps ground responses in factual sources, constitutional AI methods reduce harmful outputs, and model distillation creates smaller, more efficient versions of large models without significant quality loss.

The Future of AI

The field of AI is evolving rapidly. Multimodal models that understand text, images, audio, and video together are becoming mainstream. AI agents that can use tools, browse the web, and complete complex tasks autonomously are emerging. And the push toward smaller, more efficient models promises to democratize access to AI capabilities across devices and platforms.
